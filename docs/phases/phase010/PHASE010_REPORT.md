## Summary

building, placed on a free grass block, and two open recipes work there: `dig_grave` (needs the worker's

## Mechanics

Corpse. When a resident dies (`_record_death_event`), a ToolItem of kind `corpse` is created on the
cell where they died. It weighs 60, records `resident`, `household`, and `died_period`, and is not
buried. It is an ordinary physical object: `pick_up` takes it from the same cell, `drop_item` sets it
down, and the carrier's load is the usual carry state. A person's load limit is 50, so the 60 kg corpse
is overloaded (slower) but does not fall until the load passes 100. No drag or cart mechanic exists, so a
corpse moves only with someone carrying it. The world does not move or bury corpses on its own.

Dig. `dig_grave` is an open recipe with `at_building: graveyard` and `burial: dig`. A worker can attempt
it only with their own spade in hand and while standing inside the graveyard footprint. On completion it
adds one open grave to the graveyard building's `open_graves` count and yields one `grave_spoil`
(bulk, to the town store, the engine's convention for bulk output).

Bury. `bury` is an open recipe with `at_building: graveyard`, `burial: bury`, and one `grave_spoil`
input (backfill). It needs an open grave (`open_graves >= 1`) and a corpse on the graveyard ground, or
carried by a worker inside the graveyard. The earliest-dead unburied corpse there is the one buried.
On completion the corpse is detached from the map and any hand, marked `buried = 1`, and kept as a
record. The grave count drops by one, and a `burial` event is recorded.

Coffin. `make_coffin` is a woodworker recipe (3 timber, saw, at the woodshop). Bury neither requires
nor consumes a coffin. A coffin has no mechanical effect; it exists as an item only.

Location and waiting. A located recipe is refused outside its building (`_can_attempt`). A job that is
already running waits, armed with its inputs, while the worker is outside the building or a bury's
grave or corpse has gone (`_resolve_jobs`). The job completes once the state holds.

Cognition boundary. The world publishes the recipes and the corpse's location, validates the preconditions,
and applies the effects. It does not choose who digs, carries, or buries. A resident's backend chooses
among the offered options. A test checks that a corpse left alone stays put.

## Risk rule

A household member is at risk when a corpse of their household is unburied and at least
`BURIAL.risk_after_periods` (3) periods old. `burial_risk(household)` returns `onset_chance` (0.05)
then, and 0.0 before. The risk ends when the corpse is buried. Each dawn (the day rollover in `tick`, with
the metabolism subsystem on), each living member without a `disease` draws a stateless value
`random.Random(f"{seed}:burial:{name}:{day}").random()`. If it is below the chance, the member is
`diagnose`d with `fever`, which is the existing disease mechanism: `disease` is set, an Illness node is
recorded, and the existing daily rule drains `health_value`. No new health mechanism was added, and the
draw keeps no RNG state on the world.

## Limits

- The corpse does not decay, so it never rots or smells. Only the household risk models its effect.
- The risk reaches only household members, by the household attribute. A corpse in the open does not
  affect neighbours.
- The onset happens on the dawn tick (`tick` day rollover), with metabolism on. `pass_period` alone
  does not roll the draw.
- Bury backfills with `grave_spoil` from the town store, not from the worker. The store is a town-wide
  pool in this engine, so a grave can be filled with spoil from anywhere. Dig's spoil also goes to the
  store.
- A coffin does nothing. Bury neither requires nor consumes one.
- Each corpse is carried by one person. The 60 kg weight is overloaded but under the drop threshold.
- A dead resident's inventory stays on their dead node (existing behaviour, not changed).
- The APPROX numbers were not checked against any source.
- The backend is not exercised: no simple or Ocelot backend chooses dig or bury in a run. The options are
  published and enacted by the world, and tested directly.

## Open questions

1. **Which corpse does bury take?** With several corpses on the graveyard ground, the world buries the
   earliest-dead one. A resident choosing a specific corpse would need a target argument, which crosses
   the boundary the other way. Decide whether the world's rule is acceptable.
2. **Should a coffin do anything?** A coffin could reduce risk or be required for a family's burial. No
   source was checked, so I left it unused.
3. **Should a corpse decay?** The item has a `decay` field, currently 0. A decay rate would need a source.
4. **Should the risk attach to place, not household?** A corpse left near a well or a home could expose
   everyone there. That is a bigger model.
5. **Verify the APPROX values** against the cited references, if sourced figures are wanted.

## Deviations

- **Bury's product row.** A recipe needs an output row. A burial has none, so its row is `["", 0]`. The
  self-test requires exactly that shape for `burial: bury` recipes, and the dominated-recipe analysis
  skips zero-output recipes. This is the only way to add a no-product recipe without a new recipe kind.
- **Warnings.** The "no inputs" warning now accepts `at_building`, so dig, which has no inputs, is not a
  warning.
- **Registry name.** The brief says `bury` and `dig_grave` are open actions. I made both open recipes, so
  they reach the existing `work` option and the job engine. The corpse registry is `_corpses`, since
  `_bodies` already holds the living residents' Body nodes. The item kind is `corpse`, not `body`.
- **Write scope.** The brief named `domains/world_affordances.py`, `domains/bigville_entities.py`,
  `worlds/bigville_world.py`, and the assets. The entity data lives in `packs/bigville/`, as your context
  said, so the pack files and the test-hamlet pack are also changed. The asset generator's source changed
  too, to add the sprite.
- **Seeds.** `generate_seeds` writes `bigville/seeds/gen_recipes.json`, which is generated and was changed.
  Its new know-how rule for `bury` reads "bury makes  x0 from a labourer", because the empty output reads
  as blank. This is cosmetic. The generator's rule text would need a special case for an empty output.
- **Other session.** The working tree had uncommitted hunks in `worlds/bigville_world.py`, and the other
  session's files. I did not touch them. The commit must include or split the two hunks listed in
  "World", as the supervisor decides.
