MONOPOLY: RULES FOR THIS TABLE

Purpose and victory

This is a negotiation and property-trading game. Players move around the board,
buy properties, collect rent, improve complete colour groups, and trade with
one another. A player who cannot meet a debt is bankrupt and leaves the game.
The last player who remains solvent wins. The participants must agree on any
minor question not answered by this rulebook before continuing, and should
record that agreement for the rest of the game.

Contents

The physical set contains one board, two six-sided dice, eight playing tokens,
twenty-eight Title Deed cards, sixteen Chance cards, sixteen Community Chest
cards, thirty-two green houses, twelve red hotels, play money in denominations
of 1, 5, 10, 20, 50, 100, and 500 dollars, and a bank tray. The bank holds
the money, Title Deeds, houses, hotels, and cards that are not currently held
by players. The bank is not a player and cannot go bankrupt. If the bank runs
out of money, the banker may make more paper money. The number of houses and
hotels is limited to the pieces in the box.

Preparing the table

Choose one person to be banker. The banker keeps the bank's money separate
from personal money, pays salaries and awards, sells houses and hotels, holds
the Title Deeds not owned by players, conducts auctions, and receives taxes,
fines, interest, and mortgage payments. The banker may also play, but must
keep the two accounts separate.

Shuffle Chance and Community Chest separately and put each deck face down on
its marked space. Put the Title Deeds, houses, hotels, bank tray, money, dice,
and tokens where everyone can reach them. Each player chooses one token and
places it on GO. The players roll the dice once; the highest result takes the
first turn, and play proceeds clockwise. Give each player 1500 dollars: two
500 notes, four 100 notes, one 50 note, one 20 note, two 10 notes, one 5 note,
and five 1 notes. The remainder stays with the bank.

The board spaces, in order from GO and moving clockwise, are: GO; Mediterranean
Avenue; Community Chest; Baltic Avenue; Income Tax; Reading Railroad; Oriental
Avenue; Chance; Vermont Avenue; Electric Company; Connecticut Avenue; Jail;
St. Charles Place; States Avenue; Virginia Avenue; Pennsylvania Railroad; St.
James Place; Community Chest; Tennessee Avenue; New York Avenue; Free Parking;
Kentucky Avenue; Chance; Indiana Avenue; Illinois Avenue; B. & O. Railroad;
Atlantic Avenue; Ventnor Avenue; Water Works; Marvin Gardens; Go To Jail;
Pacific Avenue; North Carolina Avenue; Community Chest; Pennsylvania Avenue;
Short Line; Chance; Park Place; Luxury Tax; Boardwalk.

A turn

On a turn, roll both dice and move the token clockwise by the total. Resolve
the space where the token stops before the next player begins. If the movement
passes GO, the player receives 200 dollars from the bank. A player who lands
exactly on GO also receives 200 dollars. Collect this award only once for one
complete circuit; do not collect it for being sent directly to a space beyond
GO unless the movement actually passed GO.

If both dice show the same number, the player has rolled doubles. Resolve the
move, then roll again and take another turn. After rolling doubles three times
in succession during one turn, stop immediately and move the token to Jail;
the third movement is not taken and no extra roll is granted. A player in Jail
does not receive another turn from doubles until legally released.

Buying and auctioning property

When a player lands on an unowned property, that player may pay the printed
price to the bank and take the matching Title Deed. If the player declines,
the banker must auction the property. The auction may start at any price, and
any player, including the player who declined, may bid. Bids may be increased
by any amount. The highest bidder pays the bank and receives the deed. The
banker must hold the auction even when nobody initially wants the property.

When a player lands on a property owned by another player, the owner may claim
the rent printed by the deed. The owner must ask for rent before the next
player rolls; if the owner fails to claim it in time, no rent is collected.
Rent is not collected on a mortgaged property. A player may own property while
in Jail and may still collect rent, trade, mortgage property, and buy or sell
buildings when the relevant rules allow it.

Railroads and utilities

The four railroads are Reading, Pennsylvania, B. & O., and Short Line. Each
costs 200 dollars. Rent is 25 dollars when the owner has one railroad, 50 for
two, 100 for three, and 200 for all four. The two utilities are Electric
Company and Water Works; each costs 150 dollars. Rent on one utility is four
times the dice total, and rent on both utilities is ten times the dice total.

Building houses and hotels

After owning a complete colour group, a player may buy houses from the bank
and place them on its properties. Buildings must be added evenly: a property
cannot have a second house until every property in the group has one, and no
property may be more than one house ahead of another in the same group. A
property may have at most four houses. After four houses on every property in
the group, the player may exchange four houses on one property for one hotel,
paying the same group's building cost. A hotel replaces the four houses and
is the maximum improvement. Buildings may be sold back to the bank for half
their purchase price, also evenly. A player may not build on a mortgaged
property or while any property in that colour group is mortgaged. If the bank
has no house or hotel piece available, wait until a piece is returned; do not
invent an unlimited supply.

Other spaces

Landing on Chance or Community Chest means draw the top card, read it aloud,
carry out its instruction immediately, and return it to the bottom of its deck
unless it is a Get Out of Jail Free card. A player who receives that card may
keep it, trade it, or use it in place of paying to leave Jail; return it to the
bottom of the correct deck after use. If a movement card sends a player past
GO, collect 200 dollars unless the card says otherwise.

Income Tax is paid to the bank as 200 dollars for this game. Luxury Tax is paid
to the bank as 100 dollars. Free Parking has no reward and no penalty: it is a
resting space only. Visiting Jail while landing on Jail normally has no effect.
Go To Jail sends the token directly to Jail, ends the player's turn, and does
not award 200 dollars for passing GO.

50 from the bank. Get Out of Jail Free: keep this card. Go back three spaces:

pay the bank 25 for each house and 100 for each hotel you own. Take a trip to
Reading Railroad: move there and collect 200 if passing GO. You have been
elected Chairman: pay each other player 50. Your building loan matures: take

pay 40 for each house and 115 for each hotel. You have won second prize: take

Jail

A player enters Jail by landing on Go To Jail, drawing a Go directly to Jail
card, or rolling doubles three times in succession. Place the token on the
Jail space. To leave, the player may pay a 50-dollar fine before rolling, use
a Get Out of Jail Free card, or try to roll doubles. If doubles are rolled,
move by that roll and finish the turn without another roll. If doubles are not
rolled, try again on the next turn. After the third failed attempt, pay 50 and
move by the third roll. A player in Jail may still collect rent, trade, buy or
sell buildings, and mortgage property. Jail ends as soon as the player is
released; a released player follows the normal movement rules.

Mortgages and trades

A player may mortgage an unimproved property to the bank for the mortgage
value printed on its deed, normally half its purchase price. No rent is due on
a mortgaged property. To lift a mortgage, pay the mortgage value plus ten
percent interest to the bank. A property in a complete colour group may not be
developed while any property in that group is mortgaged. Before mortgaging a
property in a developed group, sell all buildings in that group evenly to the
bank for half price.

Players may negotiate and complete trades at any time between other players'
turns, and may trade cash, unmortgaged or mortgaged Title Deeds, and Get Out
of Jail Free cards. Both parties must agree and physically transfer the items.
Buildings are sold to the bank before a property is traded; buildings are not
traded directly between players. A mortgaged property transfers with its
mortgage, and the receiving player must either pay the bank the ten-percent
interest immediately or leave the mortgage in place and pay the interest when
lifting it. Future promises are not debts enforced by the rules unless all
players agree to treat them as ordinary conversation rather than a game asset.

Debts and bankruptcy

When a player owes money, first use cash, then sell buildings to the bank for
half price, then mortgage or trade eligible assets. If the debt is owed to
another player, transfer the remaining cash and assets to that creditor. If the
debt is owed to the bank, return the player's properties to the bank and
auction them. A player who still cannot pay is bankrupt, removes their token,
returns any retained cards as required, and takes no further turns. The player
who receives assets may use them under the normal mortgage and building rules.

Ending the game

Continue taking turns, resolving movement, purchases, rent, cards, taxes,
trades, buildings, mortgages, and debts. The game ends when all but one player
has become bankrupt. The remaining solvent player is the winner. Do not end
the game merely because one player has a large fortune, owns a particular
colour group, or has completed one circuit. A player may propose a house rule
or a timed ending, but it is not part of this rulebook unless everyone agrees
before using it.
